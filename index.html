Current Presentation

An empirical examination of whether coherence within a symbolic system can be evaluated under blinded experimental conditions.

A Blinded Discrimination Test of Coherence in Tarot as a Symbolic System

Sergio Saldivar-Salazar, MD, ScM · Enrique Saldivar, MD, PhD

This study applies a blinded discrimination framework to the analysis of tarot readings as structured symbolic material. Its purpose is not to presume or advocate a particular explanatory model, but to examine whether participants can distinguish coherent symbolic interpretations from experimentally controlled alternatives.

Investigators

Scientific and Methodological Leadership

Sergio Saldivar-Salazar, MD, ScM

Dr. Saldivar-Salazar is a physician specializing in Internal Medicine and Nephrology and a biostatistician with a long-standing career in clinical research, particularly in the evaluation of novel therapies for diabetes and metabolic diseases.

He has a sustained interest in the scientific study of anomalous and exceptional human experiences, approaching these topics from a critical, evidence-based, and methodologically rigorous perspective. He is currently affiliated as a Research Volunteer with the Division of Perceptual Studies at the University of Virginia, where he collaborates with Dr. Marieta Pehlivanova and Dr. Bruce Greyson on research related to near-death experiences.

More recently, Dr. Saldivar-Salazar has revisited an early interest in divinatory practices. His initial exposure to cartomancy dates back more than three decades, beginning with the study of the Marseille Tarot. He now investigates symbolic systems as potential tools for exploring questions of individual and collective consciousness when examined under carefully controlled experimental conditions.

Enrique Saldivar, MD, PhD

Dr. Saldivar is a physician and bioengineer with extensive experience in research, teaching, and the development of biomedical instrumentation. His academic training and professional work span medicine, engineering, and applied quantitative analysis, with a particular emphasis on experimental design, signal measurement, and system validation.

His research philosophy is grounded in methodological rigor, emphasizing reproducibility, procedural transparency, and careful control of experimental error. At the same time, he advocates an open and pragmatic approach to the empirical evaluation of unconventional hypotheses when they are clearly operationalized and investigated under appropriate scientific constraints.

In the present study, Dr. Saldivar served as the primary operator and protocol monitor, overseeing the randomization procedures, maintenance of blinding, and verification of the experimental data.

Mission

We investigate questions that span medicine, engineering, and the cognitive sciences through rigorous experimental design, quantitative analysis, and open scientific inquiry.
